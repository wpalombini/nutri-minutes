Nutri Minutes turns newly published nutrition research into short, plain-English summaries. The summaries are written by artificial intelligence and checked against each study before anything is published. They are not medical advice.

## Finding new studies

Every day we collect newly indexed studies from Europe PMC, a public database of life-science research that includes PubMed. We then keep the studies in our scope: nutrition, and exercise where it affects health, such as bones, muscle, falls, heart and metabolism, the brain and longevity. We don't cover weight-loss transformations, sports performance or appearance.

## From study to summary

Each study goes through the same steps:

1. **Grading:** we rate how much the result can be trusted. [How we grade](/how-we-grade) explains the levels.
2. **Extracting the facts:** who was studied, what was compared, what was measured and the main results, with their numbers.
3. **Writing:** a plain-English summary of what the study found, what it means and what it does not show.
4. **Fact-checking:** every sentence and every number is checked against the study. A summary with any claim the study doesn't support is not published.
5. **Checking for hype:** the summary is checked for overstated claims, such as calling a link a cause or a small effect a breakthrough. A summary with unresolved problems is not published.

## What we summarise from

We reuse a study's full text only when its licence allows it (Creative Commons Attribution, CC BY, or more open). Otherwise, including for paywalled studies, we summarise from the abstract and the study's details, in our own words. Each summary says which it used.

Every summary links to its source, through its DOI or its PubMed record, so you can read the original.

## When studies change

Journals sometimes correct or retract studies after publication. When a study we summarised is retracted, we keep the summary for the record and add a clear notice that its findings should not be relied on. When a study is corrected, we say so on its page.

## What we don't do

- We don't give medical advice. Talk to a health professional before changing your diet, exercise or medicines.
- We don't take advertising, sponsorship or affiliate money from anyone.
